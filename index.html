Hi, I'm Scott Kirk. I'm the Broker and Owner of Weichert, Realtors - Sierra Pacific Group here in Folsom. I've sold homes in this town for 16 years. And there is one number that surprises almost every buyer in escrow. It isn't the price.

It isn't the mortgage payment. It's the yearly cost of owning the house once you have it. Property tax, Mello-Roos and HOA dues. So I built a site that adds them up for every Folsom neighborhood, from real county tax bills and real sales. This video walks you through it.

By the end you'll know what the bill looks like on the house you're thinking about, and why two houses at the same price can be eight thousand dollars a year apart. Start with the base property tax. Folsom has two rates, and the line between them is Highway 50. On the 2026-27 county bills, every north-side bill I checked showed 1.0831 percent of assessed value. Every Folsom Ranch bill, south of 50, showed 1.1921 percent.

That difference is the voter-approved bonds each area pays for. On a $765,000 home, which was the citywide median sale this past year, that's about $8,286 north of the highway and $9,119 in Folsom Ranch. About $834 a year, just from which side of the freeway you're on. One thing before we go on. Your assessed value is what you paid, not what the seller paid.

Prop 13 resets it at the sale. So if the seller bought in 2004 and pays $4,000 a year, that number means nothing to you. Your bill starts over at your price. I'll come back to that when we get to the supplemental bill. Here's the calculator.

Pick a neighborhood, type a price. I'll pick Russell Ranch, in Folsom Ranch, and put in $765,000. It gives you three lines. Base tax at the 1.1921 rate. Then the Mello-Roos and district charges from a real 2026-27 bill in that neighborhood.

Then typical HOA dues where most of the sales had an HOA. Russell Ranch runs about $195 a month in dues, and the sample bill carried $2,942 in district charges. Now change the neighborhood to Willow Creek, north of 50. Same price. The base rate drops, the district charges fall to about $198, and most Willow Creek homes have no HOA.

Same price, very different year. Let's talk about Mello-Roos, because that's the line people don't expect. A Mello-Roos district, the county calls it a Community Facilities District, is a special tax that pays for roads, parks, pipelines and schools in a newer area. It's a fixed amount set by a formula, usually by the size of the home, and it shows up as its own line on your county tax bill. It is not based on your price.

A smaller house can carry a bigger Mello-Roos than a larger one two streets over, depending on which district it sits in. All of Folsom Ranch is in CFD 18. That one district billed between $855 and $1,152 on the 2026-27 bills I pulled. Then most villages have a second district on top. White Rock Springs Ranch had the heaviest sample bill in town: $4,344 a year in Mello-Roos and districts.

Mangini Ranch, $3,330. Russell Ranch, $2,942. Regency at Folsom Ranch, the 55-plus community, $1,565. These are real bills, one home per neighborhood. (Correction: the sample bills are listed by neighborhood and street, not by parcel number.) North of the highway is mostly lighter, but not zero.

Lakeview Oaks and Empire Ranch are in CFD 10. The Lakeview Oaks sample bill carried $2,023 in district charges, Empire Ranch $1,209. The Parkway Phase II pays CFD 14, about $911 a year, and that one has a published end date, September 2032. Then you get to the older tracts. Lexington Hills, Briggs Ranch, Los Cerros, Cobble Hills.

Those bills showed about $110 to $125 a year, which is mostly a landscape and lighting charge. Twelve of the fifteen north-side bills I checked were under $600. People ask me, does Mello-Roos ever go away? The facilities tax ends when the bonds are paid off. The Mello-Roos page lists every district I could document, with its rate, its yearly increase if the documents state one, and the end date.

Published end dates run from 2032 for the Parkway district to 2059 for CFD 18. Newer districts can run longer. CFD 20-1 in Russell Ranch runs through 2064. And the proposed district for Alder Creek West is written to run through 2085. One more thing.

Services taxes, the ones that pay for maintenance, can continue after the bonds are paid. So read the notice. You're entitled to know all this before you buy. California Civil Code 1102.6b says the seller has to make a good faith effort to get a Notice of Special Tax from each district and hand it to you. If you get that notice after you've signed the contract, you have three days to cancel if it was handed to you in person, five if it was mailed.

I make sure my buyers have that notice in hand before they write the offer, not after. Third line, HOA dues. About half of Folsom's single-family sales this past year had an HOA: 389 of 762. For those homes the median was $145 a month. Regency at Folsom Ranch is the high end at $286, which pays for the clubhouse and the amenities.

Russell Ranch is $195, Broadstone $187, The Parkway $173. American River Canyon is $37. And some neighborhoods reported no HOA on any sale: Historic Folsom, Briggs Ranch, Prairie Oaks, Lexington Hills, Los Cerros. HOA dues are not Mello-Roos. One goes to the county, one goes to the association.

A house can have both. Put it all together and you get this map. Each neighborhood is priced at its own median sale, with the base tax, the sample bill's district charges and twelve months of typical dues added up. Lakeview Oaks comes out highest, about $20,162 a year, mostly because the median sale there is over $1.5 million. White Rock Springs Ranch is $16,775 at a $922,000 median, and that's the Mello-Roos talking.

Historic Folsom is around $9,800. Tap any neighborhood and it jumps to its row in the table. Now the bill nobody warns you about. The supplemental tax bill. When you close, the county reassesses the house to your price.

The gap between the seller's old value and your new value gets taxed for the months left in the tax year, and that bill comes straight to you. Your lender doesn't pay it from the impound account. Close in December on a $765,000 home where the seller's value was $420,000, and that first bill is roughly $1,900 north of 50 or $2,100 in Folsom Ranch. Close between January and May and you get two bills, one for the rest of this year and one for all of next. It can take up to two years to arrive.

Set the money aside at closing. If you want to check a specific house yourself, here's how. Get the parcel number off the listing or the disclosures. Search it on the Sacramento County property tax site. Open the current bill and read the direct levies section, line by line.

Every district is named. Or text me the address and I'll pull the bill, read every line, check the HOA from the listing, and send you the yearly cost on one page. No charge, no meeting. Before you write an offer: the supplemental tax bill

After you close, the county sends a separate bill for the gap between the seller's assessed value and your price, for the months left in the tax year. Close between January 1 and May 31 and you get two. Your lender's impound account usually does not pay it, so plan to pay it yourself.

How the estimates work

1. Base property tax

California taxes 1% of your assessed value under Prop 13. It starts at your purchase price and generally rises at most 2% a year after that. New construction, a change in ownership, or the end of a temporary decline-in-value reduction can raise it more. Voter-approved bonds add more. On 2026-27 bills, Folsom Ranch adds Folsom Cordova school bonds (0.01737657% and 0.15853099%) and Los Rios College (0.01616145%), for 1.19206901%. North of Highway 50 the second school bond is smaller (0.04957422%), for 1.08311224%. Same rate on every bill we checked in each area.

2. Mello-Roos and district charges

A Mello-Roos district, or Community Facilities District (CFD), pays for roads, parks, pipelines and other public facilities in a new area. The tax is a fixed amount set by a formula, usually by home size, not by your price. Landscape and lighting districts add smaller charges. All of them show under Direct Charges and Special Assessments on the county bill. Every district, with documents.

Common questions

What does it cost each year to own a home in Folsom?
Three parts. The base property tax on the 2026-27 county bills is 1.1921% of assessed value in Folsom Ranch, about $1,192 per $100,000, and 1.0831% north of Highway 50, about $1,083 per $100,000. Then district charges: on real 2026-27 bills, Folsom Ranch homes paid $1,565 to $4,344 in Mello-Roos and other districts, while 12 of the 15 north-side sample bills showed under $600. Then HOA dues where there is one. The table adds it up by neighborhood.
How much is Mello-Roos in Folsom Ranch?
On real 2026-27 county bills, Folsom Ranch homes paid $1,565 to $4,344 a year in district charges. That includes CFD 18 ($854.58 to $1,152.06), CFD 17 ($51.64 to $114.94) and a village district: CFD 23 IA3 $657.36 (Regency), CFD 20 $1,673.10 (Russell Ranch), CFD 23 IA2 $2,422.64 (Mangini Ranch) and CFD 21 $3,107.24 (White Rock Springs Ranch). Amounts depend on home size and type.
Does north Folsom have Mello-Roos?
Some of it. On 2026-27 bills, Empire Ranch and Lakeview Oaks pay CFD 10 ($1,209.20 and $2,023.32 on the sample homes), The Parkway Phase II pays CFD 14 ($911.42) and Hillcrest, in Broadstone Unit 3, pays CFD 12 ($509.98). Many older north-side neighborhoods pay only a small landscape and lighting charge, or nothing.
Why will my tax bill be higher than the seller's?
Your assessed value resets to your purchase price. The seller's bill is based on their price when they bought. You will also get a supplemental tax bill for the gap between the old and new value, for the rest of the tax year. If you close between Jan 1 and May 31, expect two supplemental bills. See how the supplemental bill is figured.
